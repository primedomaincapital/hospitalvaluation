Hospital M&A and valuation, by the numbers

Hospital transaction counts, transacted revenue, the distressed-deal share, operating margins, the count of American hospitals, closures, and the multiples buyers actually pay — from published sources.

Three different kinds of number sit behind this name, and they must not be mixed. The first kind measures deal flow: how many hospitals change hands, and for how much revenue. The second kind measures the pressure that drives the flow: operating margins, the share of hospitals losing money, and the share of deals involving a distressed party. The third kind measures what buyers pay: the EBITDA and revenue multiples that turn a hospital's earnings into a price. The first kind tells a reader how busy the market is. The second tells a reader why. The third tells a reader what the work of valuation is worth.

What the latest numbers show is a market that stalled in 2025 and came back hard in 2026: deal counts fell to their lowest since Kaufman Hall began tracking in 2011, transacted revenue fell to its lowest since 2018, and distress reached a record share — then the first three quarters of 2026 produced more deals than all of 2025, led by divestitures and mega mergers rather than by distress alone.

How to read these numbers

Included: Kaufman Hall's quarterly and annual hospital transaction counts and transacted revenue, as reported by Becker's Hospital Review, Healthcare Dive, Fierce Healthcare, HealthLeaders Media, and Health Capital Topics; Kaufman Hall's National Hospital Flash Report operating-margin figures, as reported by Becker's; the American Hospital Association's hospital counts; a Harvard study of hospital closures; FOCUS Investment Banking's published healthcare multiples; and the Private Equity Stakeholder Project's hospital tracker. Excluded: any valuation multiple presented without its EBITDA or revenue band; any single hospital's sale price presented as a market multiple; and any forecast for a year not yet complete.

Kaufman Hall's "transacted revenue" is the annual revenue of the smaller party in each announced transaction, not the price paid — the two are different, and this page does not treat them as the same. Multiples from FOCUS are published ranges for private healthcare companies, not transaction prices for any specific hospital. Forecasts are labeled as forecasts, not actuals.
